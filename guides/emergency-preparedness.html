    01 / Overview

    What this framework covers

    Emergencies range from brief local interruptions to prolonged disasters that affect several essential services. This guide helps you judge six severity levels, identify which of nine critical systems could fail, and plan for four time periods. Those three assessments determine the stockpile layers to prepare and whether to stay safely at home or relocate. It is designed for London flats and houses; adapt it to the people, health needs, building and space available in your household.

      02 / Assess the event

      What can happen — and what can fail

      Severity describes how disruptive an incident becomes. Systems tell you which necessities are affected. A single incident can move up the scale if it spreads or safe alternatives disappear.

      Six severity levels Worst → best

      L6
      Systemic breakdown
      Multiple essential systems disrupted for weeks or months; staying may no longer be viable.
      L5
      Regional disaster
      Widespread power, water, food or transport failure; outside help may be delayed.
      L4
      Multi-service emergency
      Two or more essential services fail together for several days.
      L3
      Major single-service outage
      A critical system, such as water or electricity, is unavailable for roughly 1–7 days.
      L2
      Local disruption
      Local cuts, blocked routes or shops closed for hours to a few days.
      L1
      Minor incident
      Short interruption with working alternatives and normal public services.

      Why combined failures matter

      EXAMPLE A prolonged power outage can also interrupt lifts, water pumps, communications, refrigeration and card payments. Escalate the plan when several essential functions become unavailable.

      04 / Respond + prioritise

      Choose a response for your household

      Your decision depends on whether the property is safe, not just the disaster level or supplies remaining. Follow the authorities and your building’s fire procedures.

      Two response modes

      🏠 Stay / shelter

      Use when your home remains safe, basic needs can be met and official advice supports staying.

      DO: Check water, medicine, temperature, sanitation and reliable updates.

      🚶 Go / relocate

      Leave if directed, if the home becomes unsafe, or if essential needs cannot be met.

      DO: Choose a safe destination and viable route; do not wait for supplies to run out.

      Risk priority Rank preparations for your own home

      For each scenario, score Likelihood (L), Consequence (C) and Household vulnerability (V) from 1 (low) to 5 (high). Multiply the three scores. A higher score usually means more urgent preparation; always address cheap measures against life-threatening risks.

      L · Likelihood
      How plausible is this locally?
      C · Consequence
      How much harm or disruption?
      V · Vulnerability
      How exposed is this household?
      L × C × V = 1–125 EXAMPLE: Water outage 3 × 4 × 5 = 60 (high-rise); 3 × 4 × 3 = 36 (house). Scores are illustrative.

      Property check Why the scores differ

      🏢 High-rise flat
      Check fire strategy, stairs and lift dependence, water pumps, overheating and safe low-level storage.
      🏡 House
      Check flood exposure, exits, heating type, water shut-off and suitable indoor storage.
      👪 Both
      Count people, pets and medicines; consider mobility needs, medical devices and alternative accommodation.

      The decision point

      ACT EARLY If drinking water, sanitation, safe shelter or a critical medical need is unlikely to be maintained over the next 24 hours, organise safe relocation while routes remain usable. Immediate danger means act now. These are advisable household guidelines, not official deadlines.

      08 / Decision and reference card

      Know when to relocate

      🚨 Leave now No waiting for a time threshold

      • An evacuation order, an unsafe fire/smoke situation, dangerous flooding, a suspected gas/CO hazard or structural danger.
      • An urgent medical need that cannot be supported at home. Note and map out the nearest medical facilities. If services are available, call 999.

      ⏱ Plan to leave early Review at least morning + evening

      • Less than 24 hours of safe drinking water remains and no credible replacement supply is available.
      • Toilet/sanitation is unusable; indoor temperatures (e.g. cold) threaten health; power-dependent medical needs cannot be met.
      • Lifts, stairs, entry systems or travel routes prevent a safe stay or departure; local disorder threatens the home.
      DECISION POINT Do not wait until day 3, 7 or 14 just because supplies remain. If essential safety cannot be maintained for the next 24 hours, arrange a safe move while routes are usable. These are household planning triggers, not official cut-offs.

      Your five-step relocation plan Fill in before an incident

      1. Destination A: trusted local contact / safe accommodation. Destination B: outside affected area.
      2. Transport: primary route and walking/public-transport alternative; check official warnings first.
      3. Carry: go bag, required medicines, documents, payment means, phone and essential pet/baby needs.
      4. Notify: contact household members and one out-of-area contact; help vulnerable members plan.
      5. Exit safely: follow evacuation directions. Only turn off utilities when safe and appropriate.
